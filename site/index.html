Why it exists

Every board wants its own listing. Every answer might be someone else's.

A first hire at a small company usually goes like this: one job description rewritten for five boards, a spreadsheet of applicants copied out of each, and two hundred answers to “why this role” that read the same. The big ATSs fix the first two for teams with a recruiter. Nobody fixes the third honestly. A detector that calls people cheats is wrong too often to trust; what helps is seeing how each answer reached the form, and reading it yourself.

Write the job once. Each board gets a listing in its own format, checked against its own rules, with its own apply link.

  • Guides for LinkedIn, Indeed, OnlineJobs.ph, Wellfound, Upwork and your careers page
  • Two listings on one board are an A/B test, compared on applicants who met the must-haves
  • It asks for pay and hours rather than inventing them

Forms that show their work

Application and screening forms, with must-haves, file questions, and a record of how each answer arrived.

  • Typed, pasted or dropped in, counted by the browser itself
  • A “likely AI” hint that always says likely
  • The form tells applicants what it records

Tiers, not a pile

Every applicant gets a proposed A, B or C tier and a reason you can check, following the job's own rubric.

  • You accept or change it; a tier you set is never overwritten
  • Import applicants from a board's CSV, never twice
  • Cost per A-tier applicant, by listing

Messages, interviews, offers

What you tell each applicant and when, every round and scorecard, and offers kept as versions.

  • Drafts you send from your own email; nothing goes out by itself
  • A scorecard per interviewer, with the evidence for each yes or no
  • A counter-offer never overwrites what you offered before

The rule it's built on

Provenance is observation, not proof.

The form records what the browser saw: how many characters were typed, pasted or dropped in, and whether a paste came right after the applicant left the page. Style signals are a weaker hint and always say “likely”. Nothing calls an applicant a cheat, and nothing declines anyone on its own. You read the answer and decide.

Run it yourself

Yours to run, and free.

Recruited is open source. It runs on one Postgres database and needs one setting, so a small team can have it up in about ten minutes and own every record in it.

Before you ask

The questions that come up.

Does it detect AI-written answers?

It records how each answer reached the form: typed, pasted or dropped in. Separately, it can mark an answer as likely written with AI when the writing shows the usual signs. That label always says “likely”, because no detector is proof, and it's a reason to ask a question on the call rather than a reason to decline.

Will it reject anyone automatically?

No. Must-have questions mark who didn't meet them, and ranking proposes a tier with a reason, but nothing moves an applicant's stage or tier without you. A tier you set is never overwritten.

Do applicants know what the form records?

Yes. The form says so before they start. It keeps counts and timings, never keystrokes or the words someone typed and deleted.

Which boards does it write for?

LinkedIn, Indeed, OnlineJobs.ph, Wellfound, Upwork and your own careers page. Each guide covers length limits, how pay is stated, what gets a listing rejected and where the apply link goes. You paste the listing into the board; Recruited doesn't post on your behalf.

Does it send email?

No. It drafts the message and keeps a record of what was sent. You send it from your own email, through your assistant's mail connector or the app's Open in email, and the applicant's page shows what they were told and when.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It's open source under the AGPL. You pay for wherever you run it, which for a small team is a small Postgres database and one app server.
